Why this section exists — the psychology
The core job this section does

Every visitor who reaches section 10 has one or two final doubts stopping them from calling — this section exists to kill those doubts by name

By section 10 the visitor has been through 9 sections of trust-building. They've seen the badges, the photos, the video, the process, the reviews, the services, the breather, and the pricing. Logically they are convinced. But there is always a small cluster of specific, practical doubts that haven't been addressed yet — things like "does my insurance cover this?", "how messy will it be?", "what if something goes wrong after they leave?" These aren't abstract fears. They are concrete, answerable questions. The FAQ is the section that answers them directly and preemptively, so the visitor never has to pick up the phone just to get basic information.

The 10 questions roofing homeowners actually ask — use these as your base
Question 1 — promote to visual section
"Does my insurance cover this?"
Fear: I'll have to pay everything out of pocket
The single most asked question in roofing. Promote this above the FAQ as its own visual section. Answer: yes for storm/hail/wind damage, you handle the claim, they pay the deductible only.
Question 2
"Will there be a huge mess on my property?"
Fear: Nails in the driveway, debris everywhere for days
Answer specifically: magnetic nail sweepers on all surfaces, full debris removal, final walkthrough before leaving. "Clean-up is included on every job — no exceptions."
Question 3
"How long will the job take?"
Fear: Workers will be on my property for a week disrupting my life
Give specific timeframes by job type: inspections take 45 minutes, repairs 1–4 hours, full replacements 1–2 days. Specificity removes the fear of an open-ended disruption.
Question 4
"What warranty do you offer?"
Fear: They'll do the job and disappear if something goes wrong
State both warranties: your workmanship warranty (10 years in writing) AND the manufacturer's material warranty (25–50 years). Mention it's transferable on home sale — this adds unexpected value.
Question 5
"Are you licensed and insured?"
Fear: Cowboy contractor who takes money and disappears or leaves me liable
Show the actual license number and both insurance types (general liability + workers' comp). Say "you can verify both at any time" — the offer to verify is itself a trust signal even if they never do.
Question 6
"Will there be hidden costs after the quote?"
Fear: Getting an invoice with surprise charges I didn't agree to
"Your written quote is the final price — no surprises. If we find unexpected damage during the job, we stop, call you, and get approval before proceeding. Nothing added without your sign-off."
Question 7
"Can I choose my own materials / shingle colour?"
Fear: Being forced into whatever is cheapest or easiest for the contractor
"Yes — we walk you through all available options at your free inspection. We work with [brands] and can match existing shingles or choose from a full colour range."
Question 8
"Do I need to be home during the work?"
Fear: Having to take time off work or rearrange my schedule
"You don't need to be home for the repair itself — just for the initial walkthrough and final inspection. We'll send you photos throughout the day and call when we're done."
Question 9
"How soon can you start?"
Fear: Being put on a waitlist for weeks while the leak gets worse
Be specific: "Inspections available same-day or next morning. Emergency repairs within 24 hours. Full replacements typically scheduled within 3–5 business days." Vague answers here kill urgency.
Question 10

Fear: My insurer and the contractor will conflict and I'll be stuck in the middle

The promotion rule — when a question gets its own visual section
From your notes — the most important FAQ rule

If one question is asked on almost every call, it leaves the FAQ and becomes its own full visual section above it

Your notes said: "If a question is asked frequently enough, turn it into its own visual section on the main page rather than hiding it in the FAQ." For roofing, the insurance question meets this bar on almost every site. It's asked so consistently and has such a high conversion impact that leaving it buried in an accordion is a significant missed opportunity. A promoted visual section gets read — an accordion item only gets clicked by visitors who think to look for it.

The promoted section appears immediately above the FAQ accordion with a teal border, a "Asked on almost every call" badge, a full answer, and trust pills. It cannot be missed. The FAQ then handles the remaining 5–7 questions below it.

Weak answer vs strong answer — the difference in conversion impact
Weak answer — adds no value

"It depends on the size and complexity of the job. Contact us for more information."
Answers nothing. Sends the visitor to a phone call just to get basic information. Frustrating, not reassuring. This is the equivalent of "it varies" — it doesn't clear the doubt, it creates a new one.
Strong answer — clears the doubt completely
"How long does a roof replacement take?"
"Most full replacements are completed in 1–2 days. We start at 7am, work straight through, and do a full clean-up before leaving. You'll have a weather-tight roof by day one evening."
Specific timeframe, specific start time, clean-up mentioned, weather protection addressed. Every fear about the timeline is killed in three sentences. The visitor has nothing left to wonder about.

The rule for every FAQ answer

Read each answer and ask: "After reading this, does the visitor still have any version of this question?" If yes, the answer is incomplete. A good FAQ answer is one where the question genuinely disappears after reading it — not one that redirects to a phone call. "Contact us for more details" is never an acceptable FAQ answer. If the answer requires a call, the question doesn't belong in the FAQ.

Your FAQ should never be "finished"

Your notes said to treat the FAQ as a continuously updated conversion tool, not a static page element. Every month you should review discovery call notes and add any new questions that appeared more than twice. Every quarter, review which FAQ items are clicked most (using heat maps) and consider promoting the top ones to visual sections. A FAQ built in month one from guesses will always underperform a FAQ built in month six from 50 real discovery calls.

Why the FAQ goes after pricing and before the final CTA

Pricing creates new questions. The moment a visitor sees "full replacement — free estimate" they immediately think "what does that typically cost?", "is there a warranty?", "will insurance help?" These are exactly the questions the FAQ answers. Placing the FAQ before pricing means it can't address the questions that pricing raises. Placing it after pricing means every question the visitor formed while reading the tier cards gets answered before they hit the final CTA. They arrive at section 11 with zero remaining doubts — which is the exact mental state that produces the highest-converting final CTA clicks.
