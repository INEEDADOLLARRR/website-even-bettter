Why this section exists — what it does psychologically
The core reason

Photos prove past work. Video proves a living, breathing business.

By the time a visitor reaches section 4, they've seen your trust strip, authority badges, and before/after photos. They believe you've done good work before. But there's one doubt left that photos can't kill: "Is this company still active? Will they actually answer the phone? Are they real people?" A short video of the crew working, the owner talking directly to camera, or a drone shot of a freshly finished roof answers all of that in 30 seconds. Movement is life — a static page of photos could be from 5 years ago. A video with today's weather in the background cannot be faked.

Why here: This is the peak conviction moment. Sections 1–3 built the case logically. Section 4 seals it emotionally. Your notes said "merging the convincing part with clearing doubts" is the goal — video does both at once.

Part 1 — the video itself
The single most important rule

30–60 seconds maximum — not a minute more

Roofing homeowners are busy, stressed, and comparing multiple companies. They will not watch a 3-minute company overview. The goal of this video is not to tell your full story — it's to prove you're real and make them feel confident enough to call. 30–60 seconds does that. A longer video has more chances to lose them. Keep it ruthlessly short.

Why: Your notes said "most people have an 8-second attention span." A video that auto-plays a 3-minute brand story is the video nobody watches. A tight 45-second clip of a crew finishing a beautiful roof is the one that gets watched to the end.
The 3 video types — pick one or combine
Drone shot
Drone flyover of finished roof

A slow aerial reveal of a freshly completed roof. Start wide showing the neighborhood, push in on the roof. Incredibly professional-looking with a cheap consumer drone.

Best for: showing the full scope of large replacement jobs and making the work look impressive.
Crew in action
Crew working on a live job

Handheld or tripod footage of the crew installing shingles, cutting materials, working together. Branded shirts visible. Real job site sounds — no music needed.

Best for: proving a real team exists and showing the quality of the physical process, not just the result.
Owner to camera
Owner speaking directly to viewer

The owner standing at a job site, speaking plainly: who they are, what they guarantee, why they started the company. No script — conversational, honest, direct.

Best for: high-ticket sales where trust in a specific person matters. Most powerful trust signal possible.

The best option if you can do only one

Combine the owner-to-camera opening (10 seconds of "Hi, I'm [Name], owner of [Company], and I want to show you what we do") with crew footage (30 seconds of the team working) and a drone closing shot of a finished roof (10 seconds). That 50-second video hits all three trust triggers: a real person, a real process, and a real result. Film it with a modern smartphone — you do not need a professional videographer.

The 60-second script structure — shot by shot

60-second roofing video structure

0–8 sec
Owner opens to camera at job site
"Hi, I'm [Name], owner of [Company]. For 20 years we've been protecting homes in [City] — and I want to show you exactly how we work." Outdoors, branded shirt, real job site behind them.
8–30 sec
Crew footage — real work, real team
B-roll of crew installing shingles, measuring, cutting, working together. No narration needed here — let the work speak. Branded shirts visible in every shot. Natural job site audio.
30–48 sec
Drone or wide shot of a completed roof
A slow aerial reveal or wide-angle ground shot of a finished, clean roof. If drone isn't available, a wide-angle shot from the street of a recently completed job works just as well.
48–60 sec
Owner closes — single direct CTA
"If your roof needs attention, call us today — we offer a free inspection and a same-day quote." Show phone number as text overlay. End on owner's face, not a logo card.
Part 2 — the unique value proposition (beside the video)
The most important rule for the value prop

Specific promise only — "We fix roofs" is a conversion killer

Your notes were direct: "We fix roofs gives zero reason to choose you." Every roofer on the planet fixes roofs. The value proposition is the specific, concrete, verifiable promise that answers the question "Why should I call YOU instead of the other guy?" It cannot be vague. It cannot use words like "professional," "quality," "reliable," or "experienced" — those are adjectives every competitor uses too.

The test: Read your value prop out loud and ask "could my competitor say this exact sentence?" If yes, rewrite it.
Generic — kills conversion

Empty words any roofer can claim. No specific promise, no reason to choose you, no verifiable commitment. The visitor's brain files this as "just another roofer."

"We provide quality roofing services with professional results and great customer service."
Specific — drives conversion

A concrete, verifiable, unique promise. The visitor immediately knows what sets you apart and what they're committing to if they call.

"24-hour emergency response · 10-year workmanship guarantee · same-day free inspection, every time."
Strong value prop bullets — real examples to model
Speed-based promise

"Emergency repairs within 24 hours — guaranteed"

Time is the #1 stressor for a homeowner with a leaking roof. A guaranteed response window converts faster than any other promise because it directly answers their biggest anxiety.

Only use this if it's genuinely true. A broken promise destroys reviews.
Warranty-based promise

"10-year workmanship guarantee — in writing"

"In writing" is the power phrase. It transforms a claim into a contract. Most roofers offer warranties verbally — putting it in writing makes you immediately different.

Pair with the manufacturer's material warranty (e.g. 25-year GAF shingle) to create a "double guarantee" that removes all perceived risk.
Cleanliness-based promise

"No-mess guarantee — we clean up completely or we come back"

Homeowners dread nails in the driveway and debris in the garden. A no-mess guarantee is highly specific, easy to visualize, and almost nobody else offers it. Instant differentiator.

Your notes said to remove all doubt. "What about the mess?" is one of the most common unspoken doubts in roofing.
Insurance-based promise

"We handle all insurance claims for you — at no extra cost"

For storm damage jobs especially, dealing with insurance adjusters is the homeowner's nightmare. If you handle the paperwork, say it explicitly. This is a massive friction remover for storm season leads.

This is a free differentiator for any roofer who already does this but never says it on their site.
Words to ruthlessly cut from your value prop copy
Professional Quality Reliable Experienced Seamless Trusted Cutting-edge Innovative Empowering Solutions Best-in-class World-class

Why these words kill conversion — from your notes

Your notes said: "Jargon creates cognitive load — simple language creates trust via Cognitive Fluency." These words are not just vague — they actively make the brain work harder to extract meaning, and when no meaning arrives, trust drops. Every one of these words is something every competitor also claims. Replace each one with a specific number, timeframe, guarantee, or action. "Reliable" becomes "we answer the phone 7am–8pm, 7 days a week." "Quality" becomes "10-year workmanship guarantee in writing."
