The friction remover · placed right after the video because that's when the visitor asks
"OK I'm interested — but what actually happens if I call?"

Why this section exists — the psychology
The core problem it solves

A roof is unfamiliar, expensive, and disruptive — homeowners are scared of the unknown

Most homeowners have never hired a roofer before. They don't know what happens after they click the button. Will a salesperson show up and hard-sell them? Will they get locked into something? Will workers be in their house? How long will it take? How messy will it be? All of these unspoken fears live in the gap between "I need a roofer" and "I'll actually call one." This section closes that gap completely by making the entire process visible, predictable, and safe-feeling before they commit to anything.

From your notes: "Removes uncertainty about the process — prospects need to know what happens next before they commit." This is the section that takes a hesitant visitor and turns them into a caller.

Why it goes here — after video, before reviews

By section 5 the visitor is emotionally convinced (video did that) but hasn't committed yet. The "How It Works" section is the bridge between conviction and action. It comes before the deep reviews because it answers a more urgent question — "what do I actually do next?" — and reviews are there for people who still have lingering doubts after they understand the process. Get the process clear first, then pile on more social proof.

Each step — what to write and why
1
Book a free inspection

This step must answer the homeowner's first and biggest fear: "If I contact them, am I committing to anything?" The word "free" and "no obligation" need to appear either in the step title or immediately below it. The CTA here is low-risk — you're not asking them to buy, you're asking them to let you look. That's a much smaller commitment and converts far more easily.

Also add a timeframe. "Same-day or next morning" tells them they won't be waiting a week. For storm damage especially, speed is everything.

Copy that converts
"Book a Free Roof Inspection — we come to you, no sales pressure, no obligation. Available same-day."
Copy that kills conversion
"Contact us to schedule a consultation." — No "free," no timeframe, sounds like a formal business meeting. Adds friction instead of removing it.
2
Get a detailed quote

This step answers the second biggest fear: "Will I understand what I'm paying for or will it be a confusing invoice I can't read?" The word "detailed" is doing real work here — it signals transparency. Add "written" if you give written quotes, because verbal quotes feel unreliable. Add a timeframe again: "within 24 hours" tells them it won't drag on.

Mention that the quote covers exactly what needs doing — this preemptively kills the fear of being upsold on work that isn't needed. For insurance jobs, you can replace this step with "We handle your insurance claim" — which is an even stronger friction remover for storm damage leads.

Copy that converts
"Get a written breakdown of exactly what needs fixing, what it costs, and how long it takes — within 24 hours of your inspection."
Insurance variation (storm season)
"We work directly with your insurance adjuster and handle all the paperwork — at no extra cost to you."
3
Enjoy a leak-free home

This is the outcome step — the desired result the homeowner actually wants. Notice it's not "We complete the job" or "Installation is finished." Those describe your action. "Enjoy a leak-free home" describes their outcome — the feeling of relief and safety they're actually buying. Always frame step 3 around the customer's life after the work, not around your process.

Add "clean-up included" explicitly. Mess is one of the most common unspoken fears — nails in the driveway, debris in the garden. Saying "we clean up completely" removes a real objection. Add a timeframe: "most jobs done in 1–2 days" tells them their life won't be disrupted for a week.

Copy that converts
"We handle everything from start to finish — including full clean-up. Most jobs completed in 1–2 days. You just enjoy a dry, protected home."
Copy that kills conversion
"We complete your roofing project." — Describes the contractor's action, not the homeowner's outcome. Bland, forgettable, zero emotional pull.
The section headline — the most overlooked element
Don't call it "How It Works"

The section headline sets the emotional tone before they read a single step

Most roofers title this section "How It Works" or "Our Process." Technically correct, completely forgettable. The headline is a conversion opportunity. It should either remove fear, set an expectation of ease, or make a direct promise — before the homeowner even reads step 1.

A better headline pre-answers the question the visitor is silently asking as they scroll into this section.
Generic: "How It Works" or "Our Process"
Better: "Here's exactly what happens when you call us"
Better: "Getting your roof fixed is simpler than you think"
Better: "From your first call to a leak-free home — 3 steps"
Best for storm damage: "Storm damage? Here's what we do — starting today"

The 5 silent fears this section kills — homeowners never say these out loud

Unspoken doubts cleared by each element of the 3 steps

"If I contact them am I committing to buy?"
Killed by "free inspection, no obligation" in step 1. Removes the fear of being trapped.
"Will a pushy salesperson show up?"
Killed by "no sales pressure" phrasing in step 1. Names the fear and dismisses it directly.
"Will I understand what I'm paying for?"
Killed by "detailed written quote" in step 2. "Written" signals accountability and transparency.
"Will my house be a mess for days?"
Killed by "clean-up included, most jobs 1–2 days" in step 3. Timeframe + cleanliness promise.
"How long will this whole thing take?"
Killed by timeframes on every step: "same-day," "within 24 hours," "1–2 days." No mystery left.
Do this / never do this
Do this
  • Exactly 3 steps — not 4, not 5
  • Put "free" and "no obligation" in step 1
  • Add a timeframe to every step
  • Frame step 3 as the customer's outcome
  • Write the section headline as a promise
  • Say "clean-up included" explicitly
  • Use icons — one per step, simple and clear
  • Keep each step to 1 sentence max
  • Add a CTA button directly below the steps
  • Choose the variation that matches your main service
Never do this
  • Use more than 3 steps — it feels complex
  • Title it just "How It Works" or "Our Process"
  • Write steps that describe your actions not their outcomes
  • Skip timeframes — vagueness creates anxiety
  • Use paragraphs — steps must be scannable
  • Leave out "free" and "no obligation"
  • Use complicated icons that need decoding
  • Put this section at the bottom of the page
  • Have no CTA button after the steps
  • Write steps in passive voice ("you will be contacted")
The CTA that goes directly below the steps

Every "How It Works" section must end with a CTA button directly below the 3 steps. The visitor just learned the process is easy and free — strike while the iron is hot. Don't make them scroll to find the button.

The rule from your notes: one primary goal per section

This entire section has one job — convert a hesitant visitor into someone who picks up the phone or clicks the button. Every word, every timeframe, every "no obligation" phrase is engineered toward that one moment. Don't add secondary links, don't add navigation, don't add a gallery. Three steps, one headline, one CTA button. That's the whole section.
