Section 12 — The footer

The last element on the page · not a dumping ground · a final trust reinforcer and SEO powerhouse
Most roofers treat the footer as an afterthought — done right it does real conversion and ranking work

Why the footer matters more than most people think
The two jobs the footer does

Job 1: catches visitors who scrolled past section 11 without acting · Job 2: sends critical signals to Google about your local legitimacy

The footer is the last chance to convert a visitor who scrolled all the way to the bottom and still hasn't clicked. More importantly, it is one of the most SEO-significant elements on the page — Google reads the footer on every page of your site and uses the NAP data (Name, Address, Phone) to verify your local business listing, match your Google Business Profile, and rank you in local map searches. A footer that has inconsistent contact info, is missing schema markup, or has a cluttered navigation actually hurts both conversions and rankings simultaneously. Built correctly, it does two jobs at once without any extra effort.

From your notes: "Add Local Business Schema Markup — implement JSON-LD structured data specifying the business name, address, phone, service area, and hours. Enables Google Maps and local search results."

Anatomy — every column, every element, every reason
Dark background
The footer uses the same dark background as the section 8 breather — a visual bookend that signals "we've reached the end." It creates a clear boundary between the page content and the footer, and feels premium rather than like the page just ran out.
Use #1a1a18 or similar warm dark gray — same value as section 8 creates visual consistency across the page.
Column 1 — NAP
Name, Address, Phone — the single most important SEO element in the footer. These three pieces of data must exactly match your Google Business Profile listing, your Yelp listing, and every other directory listing. One character difference between your website footer and your Google Business Profile can hurt your local ranking. Include the full street address, city, state, zip, and your license number.
Company Name · Full address · Phone number · Email · License # — all visible, all matching every other listing exactly.
Column 1 — hours
Business hours displayed plainly next to or below the phone number. Removes the fear of "what if I call and nobody answers?" For emergency roofing situations especially, knowing you're available Saturday at 7am makes a homeowner call instead of search for another company.
"Mon–Sat 7am–7pm · Same-day available" — specific days and times, not "we're always here for you."
Column 2 — navigation
A clean list of 6–8 essential links. Not a full site map. Not every blog post. The links should cover your main service pages (inspection, repair, replacement), location pages (service areas), and supporting pages (about, reviews, contact). This also creates internal links that Google follows for SEO.
Home · Inspection · Repair · Replacement · Service Areas · About · Reviews · Contact — 8 links maximum.
Column 3 — trust badges
Your certification badges, license status, and insurance confirmation one final time. A visitor who skipped the trust strip at the top and landed here via a back-scroll will see these credentials. Google also reads alt text on badge images as local trust signals.
Licensed · Insured · GAF Certified · BBB A+ — actual badge images where possible, plain text labels otherwise.
Column 3 — star rating
Your Google rating and review count repeated one final time. Link it to your Google Business Profile — "View all" in a distinct color. For visitors who bypassed section 6's deep reviews, this is their last chance to verify you are trusted by real people.
"★★★★★ 4.9/5 · 200+ Google reviews · View all" — the number of reviews matters as much as the rating.
Column 3 — map embed
A Google Maps embed showing your location and service area. This does three things: proves you are a real physical business, confirms you are local to the homeowner's area, and creates a positive signal to Google's local algorithm.
Embed the Google Business Profile map directly — not just a screenshot. The actual Google Maps iframe is what matters for SEO signal.
Bottom bar
Copyright line, Privacy Policy link, Terms of Service link, and Sitemap link. The Privacy Policy and Terms are legally required in most jurisdictions if you collect any form data. The Sitemap link gives Google one more crawl entry point.

The NAP consistency rule — the most important SEO rule in this section
Critical for local SEO — most roofers get this wrong

Your Name, Address, and Phone must be character-for-character identical across every platform

Google's local algorithm cross-references your website footer NAP against every directory listing it can find — Google Business Profile, Yelp, HomeAdvisor, Angi, BBB, and dozens more. When these match exactly, Google's confidence in your local legitimacy increases and your map ranking improves. When they don't match — even small differences like "St." vs "Street," "(555) 000-0000" vs "555-000-0000," or "Roofing Co." vs "Roofing Company" — Google treats them as potentially different businesses and your ranking suffers. This is invisible to visitors but has a significant impact on how many people find you organically.

Your notes specifically flagged this: "Local Business Schema Markup — implement structured data specifying the business name, address, phone, service area, and hours. Validate with Google's Rich Results Test."
What inconsistency looks like

These count as different businesses to Google

Website: "123 Main St, [City]"
Google: "123 Main Street, [City]"
Yelp: "123 Main St., [City], [State]"
Angi: "(555)000-0000" vs "(555) 000-0000"
These look like minor variations to a human. To Google's algorithm they are signals of inconsistency that reduce confidence in the listing's accuracy.
What consistency looks like

Identical across every platform

Website: "[Company Name] Roofing"
Google: "[Company Name] Roofing"
Yelp: "[Company Name] Roofing"
Phone format: (555) 000-0000 everywhere
Pick one exact format for every piece of NAP data and use it verbatim everywhere. Copy-paste, never retype. One source of truth.
Local Business Schema markup — what it is and how to implement it
What schema markup is — explained simply

Invisible code that tells Google exactly what your business is, where it is, and who it serves

Schema markup is a block of JSON code that lives in the <head> section of your HTML — visitors never see it, but Google reads it on every crawl. It tells the algorithm: "This is a roofing business. It is located at this exact address. It serves these specific cities. It is open these hours. Here is the phone number. Here are the reviews." Without schema, Google has to infer all of this from the page text — and it sometimes gets it wrong. With schema, you are telling Google directly, which increases the likelihood of appearing in the local map pack (the three businesses shown above organic search results) and in voice search results.

Your notes: "Add invisible code for Google that says 'this business is physically located in this specific city.' It helps them show up in local map searches — critical for local service companies."
Local Business Schema — the exact JSON-LD template for a roofing company
Paste this in the <head> of your HTML. Replace every [bracket] with your real data. Validate at search.google.com/test/rich-results
<script type="application/ld+json"> { "@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "RoofingContractor", "name": "[Company Name] Roofing", "image": "https://yoursite.com/logo.png", "url": "https://yoursite.com", "telephone": "(555) 000-0000", "email": "hello@yoursite.com", "address": { "@type": "PostalAddress", "streetAddress": "123 Main Street", "addressLocality": "[City]", "addressRegion": "[State]", "postalCode": "[ZIP]", "addressCountry": "US" }, "geo": { "@type": "GeoCoordinates", "latitude": 00.0000, "longitude": -00.0000 }, "areaServed": [ "[City]", "[Neighboring City 1]", "[Neighboring City 2]" ], "openingHoursSpecification": [ { "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification", "dayOfWeek": ["Monday","Tuesday","Wednesday", "Thursday","Friday","Saturday"], "opens": "07:00", "closes": "19:00" } ], "priceRange": "$$", "aggregateRating": { "@type": "AggregateRating", "ratingValue": "4.9", "reviewCount": "200" } } </script>

The @type "RoofingContractor" is more specific than "LocalBusiness"

Always use the most specific schema type available. "RoofingContractor" tells Google exactly what category of business you are without it having to guess. This increases the chance of appearing in industry-specific searches ("roofing contractor near me") rather than just general local searches. Validate your schema using Google's Rich Results Test — it will show you any errors and confirm Google can read the data correctly. Run this validation every time you update contact information.

The 4 SEO elements that live in or near the footer
SEO Element 1
JSON-LD Schema in <head>
The schema code block placed in the HTML head. Validates with Google Rich Results Test. Tells Google your exact category, address, hours, and service area.
Type: RoofingContractor · includes areaServed, openingHours, telephone, address, aggregateRating
SEO Element 2
Internal links to service pages
Footer links to your service pages pass "link equity" from every page to your most important pages. Google crawls the footer on every page load — each internal link is an SEO signal.
Footer links to: /roof-inspection · /roof-repair · /roof-replacement · /service-areas
SEO Element 3
Service area links
A "Service Areas" link in the footer points to a page listing every city and neighborhood you serve. Each city can have its own landing page — each of which can rank for "[City] roofing contractor."
/service-areas → [City] · [Suburb 1] · [Suburb 2] · [Suburb 3] — each a separate indexable URL
SEO Element 4
XML Sitemap link
A "Sitemap" link in the bottom bar pointing to your XML sitemap file. Submit this URL to Google Search Console. Ensures every page on your site gets crawled and indexed, including location pages.
/sitemap.xml · submitted to Google Search Console · updated automatically when pages are added

The footer is where the entire website comes full circle

Look at what the footer repeats: the phone number (first shown in the hero), the trust credentials (first shown in section 2), the license number (first shown in section 2), the star rating (first shown in section 6), and the service area (mentioned in every section). This is not accidental repetition — it is deliberate reinforcement. A visitor who skipped section 2 but scrolled to the footer sees the credentials for the first time. A visitor who forgot the phone number after reading 10 sections finds it again. A Googlebot that crawls the footer on every page gets the NAP signal on every crawl. The footer is the page's foundation — unseen by most, essential to everything.
